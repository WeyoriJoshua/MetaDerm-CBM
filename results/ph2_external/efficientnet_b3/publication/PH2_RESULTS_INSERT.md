# PH² External Validation — Manuscript-Ready Results Insert

The frozen MetaDerm-CBM configuration was evaluated on the independent PH² dataset (n=200; 40 melanomas and 160 non-melanomas) across the five training seeds without retraining, recalibration, threshold optimisation, or modification of the metacognitive policy. Across seeds, the direct black-box classifier achieved AUROC 0.872 ± 0.038, balanced accuracy 0.747 ± 0.049, and melanoma sensitivity 0.520 ± 0.108. The soft concept-based reasoner achieved AUROC 0.921 ± 0.011, balanced accuracy 0.814 ± 0.012, and melanoma sensitivity 0.695 ± 0.033. External reasoner sensitivity was therefore broadly preserved relative to the internal Derm7pt evaluation, while discrimination, specificity, F1, AUPRC, Brier score, and calibration error were improved on PH². These differences should be interpreted as dataset-specific external performance rather than evidence that unseen data are generally easier or that the model will necessarily improve under arbitrary domain shift.

The frozen metacognitive gate retained a selective triage pattern on PH². Mean PREDICT coverage was 0.634 ± 0.067, QUERY rate was 0.288 ± 0.064, and ABSTAIN rate was 0.078 ± 0.026. The mean selective risk among PREDICT cases was 0.046 ± 0.010, while 0.745 ± 0.090 of black-box errors were deferred to QUERY or ABSTAIN. Class-conditional coverage remained asymmetric: benign PREDICT coverage was 0.729 ± 0.068, compared with melanoma PREDICT coverage of 0.255 ± 0.065. Accordingly, the selective mechanism is interpreted as risk-aware decision support rather than autonomous melanoma exclusion.

Partial semantic transfer was evaluated only for PH² criteria with defensible correspondence to Derm7pt. Mean AUROC across seeds was 0.734 for pigment-network atypia, 0.677 for dots/globules, 0.781 for streak presence, 0.784 for regression-area presence, and 0.872 for blue-whitish veil. The uneven concept-transfer performance indicates that external diagnostic reasoning can remain useful despite imperfect transfer of individual semantic representations.
